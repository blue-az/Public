The Serve, and the Mount for Its Sensor

Left: a real serve, with a Zepp handle sensor's measured data and firmware peak-detection edges overlaid on the video. Right: a mount sized for that class of sensor, designed live by an AI through Andrii Shylenko's kernelCAD connector — drag to spin it. They're not the same object — one's a swing, one's a bracket — shown side by side so you can hold both at once.

The serve — real sensor data
sensor (handle)

Skeleton is MediaPipe Pose Landmarker, run frame-by-frame on this clip. The Zepp sensor clips onto the racket's butt cap, not the wrist — so the amber marker isn't the raw wrist landmark. It's extrapolated 18% of the forearm length past the wrist, continuing the elbow→wrist line, to approximate where the handle actually extends beyond the hand. Racket hand (right) picked automatically by which wrist moved more across the swing. Ball speed (80.9 mph) appears only after contact (~1.8s).

The mount — kernelCAD's real geometry

−35°

Press play — this rotation is the real measured orientation of the Zepp handle sensor for this exact swing, frame by frame.

46 × 36 × 14 mm. The mount tumbles the way the sensor actually tumbled during your serve — 797 frames pulled from the raw Zepp capture (ztennis.db, swing 1767490069922), not a decorative spin. The camera-angle slider only orbits your viewpoint; it doesn't move the part.

Known caveat, carried over from the repo's own notes: which sensor axis maps to which physical handle direction is a documented best guess, not oracle-verified — the tumbling is real, the exact facing may be off by a fixed rotation.
