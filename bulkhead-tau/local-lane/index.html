RTX 3090 vs Ryzen AI MAX 390

Chip-to-chip local field. Same blobs, two accelerators. 26b is still the seat on both. Not a frozen paper.

What this is

Personal benchmarking of large local models on two chips I actually use. Correctness is the local-lane L2 battery under opr. Speed is decode rate (eval_count / eval_duration) on both. Nothing here is UID-verified or registered as a claim. It will move.

The Qwen3.6 → 3.8 drop has its own page: 3.6 vs 3.8. Faster, not measurably more correct. This page is the field.

Two chips

NVIDIA RTX 3090

24 GB dedicated GDDR6X. Stock TDP 350 W, capped at 320 W. Every 3090 rate is therefore conservative. Q4, flash attention, KV q8_0. Machine shorthand in the logs: desktop.

AMD Ryzen AI MAX 390

Radeon 8050S, 24 threads, 27 GB unified LPDDR5X. Vulkan (RADV GFX1151), not ROCm. The box is a Flow Z13; the comparison is the MAX 390, not the chassis. 35b is 100% iGPU here. 26b is too, on the current stack (the old 16% CPU row is stale). Latest 35b/26b decode is AC / balanced / powersave — could not switch to performance without sudo. Battery / power-saver once made 26b look 3.4× slow. Write the power state down or throw the number out.

Power ranking — RTX 3090 vs Ryzen AI MAX 390

Correction — 7 Sep 2026. The ranking below is superseded and three of its load-bearing numbers are wrong. It is retained unaltered as the published record; do not cite it.

  • The speed baseline is wrong. Every Speed figure here is normalised against gemma4:26b at 133.0 tok/s. That figure was measured on the unpinned base tag, whose default context is 262,144 — the KV cache takes the model to 39.3 GB, past one 24 GB card, so it splits across both at a 35% decode penalty. Pinned to one card it is 226 tok/s (n=6, 224.4–229.0). The whole Speed column is normalised against a two-card split figure.
  • The speed axis measures the wrong thing. Decode tok/s does not predict task completion time. gemma4:26b decodes 2.9× faster than qwen3.8:27b and finishes the same task 1.5× slower, because it emits 7,004 output tokens to 27b's 1,362. The protocol now uses measured task wall clock.
  • The L1 claim inverts under correct configuration. The text below reads “26b is 14/18 and 3.8 is 7/18 (p=0.04)”. Those trials ran on unpinned tags with thinking on. Pinned with thinking off, on the same fixtures and machine, it reverses: gemma4:26b 66.7% (40/60) against qwen3.8:27b 88.3% (53/60).

The seat has moved to qwen3.8:27b (operator decision, 7 Sep 2026). gemma4:26b is the least reliable model in the clean field at 83.7%.

No corrected cross-chip table is offered. The MAX 390 has 96 trials and none in the corrected configuration, so a like-for-like two-chip ranking cannot currently be produced. The table below this note is RTX 3090 only.

Quality rank transfers between chips. Speed rank does not. Both machines put qwen3.6:35b and qwen3.8:27b at ceiling with gemma4:26b below — on a discrete GPU and on an integrated APU. A third machine (RTX 2080, 8 GB) agrees for the one model tested there. The practical consequence: measure quality once per model, speed once per machine.

There is no chip multiplier. The MAX 390 penalty ranges 1.95×–3.40× and reverses between tasks — gemma4:26b is the least penalised on the short task and the most on the long one. Any single “N times slower” figure for this chip is wrong.

Decode is not the story on the constrained chip. On the MAX 390 qwen3.8:27b decodes at 22.4 tok/s against the two MoE models' 53–55 — a 2.4× deficit — and finishes the same work at the same speed, because it emits far fewer tokens. An earlier version of this page's data argued dense models are punished on this chip; corrected, the decode penalty and the token-economy advantage almost exactly cancel.

Stated confound: the MAX 390 runs ollama 0.32.13 against the desktop's 0.32.12. That affects cross-chip magnitudes, not within-chip ordering. MAX 390 quality is an /18 floor-scale score (n=6/fixture), not the deeper desktop corpus, and is shown as a rate so the two are comparable without implying a shared denominator.

gemma4:26b across three cards — and the default that costs everyone throughput

Same model, same probe (num_ctx 16384, temperature 0, 128-token generation), ollama 0.32.12. Decode tok/s, measured with nothing else resident.

The most useful thing on this page, if you run this model: pin num_ctx. gemma4:26b ships with a default context of 262,144. Unpinned, the KV cache takes it from 19.3 GB to 39.3 GB, and what happens next depends entirely on your hardware: on a two-card box it splits across both and costs ~35% of decode; on a single 24 GB card it does not fit at all and spills to CPU. That is the likely reason published figures for this model vary so widely — most of them are measuring the default.

The 226 figure is not a favourable slice. It holds at 128, 512 and 2048-token generations (221.2 / 214.6 / 217.9) and gives 203.7 tok/s end-to-end at 2048 tokens including prompt processing. It is also the only rate consistent with the arithmetic: 25.2B at Q4_K_M is ~15.2 GB of weights, and a 3090's 936 GB/s caps a dense model of that size near 62 tok/s. Measuring 220 is 3.6× above that ceiling, which is only possible because 8 of 128 experts are active per token — roughly 4.2 GB read per token, not 15.2.

The resident field

Models that run on both chips at a usable context. Resident is what must sit in memory (all weights, MoE included) — the capacity wall. Active is what decode actually reads per token — the bandwidth wall. MoE moves the second and not the first, which is the whole reason 26b is 3.9× the 31b decode on the same card. 26b is MoE (25.2B total / 3.8B active). 31b is dense. Both Qwen 27Bs are dense. 35b is 35B-A3B MoE: 4% lip on the 3090, 100% iGPU on the MAX 390.

The 3090 lip — not a MAX 390 problem

35b is ranked above. This section is the placement story. 23 GB of weights on 24 GB dedicated is a 4% lip. The same blob on 27 GB UMA is 100% iGPU. Nano is still unranked (0/18).

4–7% spill is not a decode cliff. 3090 35b is ~2.3× the dense 27b and ~2.5× 31b. 16k → 32k cost ~3% decode; the CPU fraction did not grow. On the MAX 390 the 4% is gone and 35b is slightly faster than 26b (59.2 vs 55.2).

35b did not win merit on either chip. E9: 14/30 (3090) and 18/30 (MAX 390) vs 26b 24/30 twice. It still fails constant-and-callers 0/6. Usable. Not the seat.

Nano failed all 18 cells by missing the implementation, not by timing out. Cells died in 2.3–2.5s. Decode class does not predict L2.

Why 26b was the seat on both chips (superseded 7 Sep 2026 — see correction above)

Tied with 31b on L2. Far faster on the 3090 (133 vs 34). On the MAX 390 it is still the interactive pick (55 t/s, 24/30). 35b is the speed pick on the MAX 390 (59 t/s, 100% GPU) and loses the battery (18/30) on one fixture. At L1 — the prompt a human actually types — 26b is 14/18 and 3.8 is 7/18 (p=0.04). Nano is a speed demon that does not implement the task.

Published cards put 31b a notch above 26b. That can be true and still not be the pick on either chip.

What this does not say

  • It does not say spill is free. It says a few percent on this meter is not the old 31b-era disaster.
  • It does not treat 35b’s 18/30 on the MAX 390 as a seat. Same-run 26b is 24/30. The miss is constant-and-callers.
  • It does not claim locals have reached current frontier. A Flash-class oneshot still clears the ceiling item the field sits under. See 3.6 vs 3.8.
  • MAX 390 26b/35b decode (17 Aug) is AC / balanced. Older Qwen 27B rows are AC / performance @8k. Battery is a different machine.
