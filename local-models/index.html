When one card is not enough

Measured 28-29 Sep 2026 on two RTX 3090s in a headless test bench, one card against both. Most of the roster fits one 24 GB card and gains nothing from a second. Two models do not fit, and they behave differently.

ModelSizeOne cardTwo cards
qwen3-next (80B MoE)50 GB46% in VRAM, 25.6 tok/s92-96% in VRAM, 72.6 tok/s
nemotron-3.5-lightning (30B MoE)25.4 GB87.5% in VRAM, 49-58 tok/s100% in VRAM, 75-88 tok/s

qwen3-next needs both cards. On one it runs at about a third of the speed with more than half the model on the CPU. nemotron-3.5-lightning is borderline: its weights nearly fit one card, but with its context and working memory about an eighth spills to the CPU. It still runs there at roughly two-thirds of its two-card speed, and holds that speed as context fills (75 tok/s at 115k tokens on two cards).

On the same five repair tasks, with every sampling parameter pinned identical, nemotron-3.5-lightning, qwen3-next Instruct and qwen3.6:35b scored 30, 27 and 29 of 30. At six trials per task that is a tie, not a ranking. Where they differ is wall clock: 8.6, 25.0 and 5.7 minutes for the thirty cells.

Earlier qwen3-next results on this site used the Thinking variant run in non-thinking mode. They describe that configuration, not the model.
